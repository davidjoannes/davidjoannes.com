#117: Reaching Generation Alpha: Shane Pruitt & Shelly Melia on Real Faith in a Digital World

September 15, 2026

On this episode of Missions Pulse, David Joannes welcomes two incredible guests: Shane Pruitt and Shelly Melia.

Shane is the Next Gen Director at NAMB and the author of 9 Common Lies Christians Believe, Calling Out the Called, Revival Generation, and Not My Jesus.

Dr. Shelly Melia serves as the Associate Dean of the Graduate School of Ministry and Professor of Childhood Education at Dallas Baptist University. Before DBU, she served as a children's and family minister for over 25 years at churches in Oklahoma, Florida, and Texas. She is also a licensed professional counselor, an editor and contributor for Children and Salvation, and co-authored the newly released book Reaching Generation Alpha with Shane Pruitt.

Shane Pruitt has spent years running diagnostics on youth ministries, and he says the question everyone used to ask — what percentage of our students are being discipled? — is now the wrong question entirely. In this episode of Missions Pulse, Shane and Dr. Shelly Melia, co-authors of the newly released Reaching Generation Alpha: A Ministry Guide for Discipling the Church Beyond the Screen, make the case that every single kid in Generation Alpha is already being discipled. The only question left is by whom — a pop star, an algorithm, TikTok, or the church.

That reframe sits at the center of the conversation. Shelly points out that Gen Z was shaped by technology — social media arrived and changed them. Gen Alpha is different: they're being formed by technology, from the moment mom or dad first hands them a tablet. Add artificial intelligence into that mix, and you get a generation that, according to Shane, doesn't know who or what it can trust anymore — not even its own eyes, since the video might be AI, the image might be generated, the "friend" might be a bot. Into that vacuum, he argues, the church has a strange kind of opportunity: to be the one thing that's still real. Not perfect, not polished, but honest — a community that can be trusted precisely because it admits where it's messy.

Shelly is candid about where that trust has to be rebuilt first: inside the home. She and Shane cite research suggesting parents are largely unaware of what their kids are doing with AI, and unaware of how early exposure to pornography — and the ability to generate explicit content themselves — has crept into ordinary childhood. Her counsel isn't panic; it's presence. Kids don't need a lecture, she says, they need someone willing to sit down and ask, "What questions do you have?" instead of just telling them what to think. Shane's own house rule with his six kids is blunter still: nothing is off-limits, bring us the awkward questions, bring us the "cringy" ones — because if they don't bring them home, they'll go find the answers somewhere else, and "we're probably not gonna like where they go."

What makes the episode land isn't just the diagnosis, though — it's the refusal to let parents off the hook by demanding perfection instead. Shane pushes back hard on the idea that kids need flawless role models. They don't. They need adults who are visibly following someone who is perfect, and who are honest about their own failures along the way. "The greatest thing you can do for your kids," he says, "is to follow the one who is perfect" — discipleship out of the overflow of your own walk with Jesus, not out of guilt for the ways you've already fallen short.

Underneath all of it is a quieter theological point that gives the whole conversation its shape: this isn't ultimately about strategy, it's about identity. Generation Alpha is growing up anxious, over-stimulated, and short on real-world play and grounding — but Shane and Shelly both return to the same hope, that a generation this connected to global peers, this fluent in searching for truth, is not a lost cause but a mission field standing in plain sight. Some of the most spiritually mature people in a household today, Shane notes, are the kids — not the parents.

📖 If this conversation resonates, Shane and Shelly's book, Reaching Generation Alpha: A Ministry Guide for Discipling the Church Beyond the Screen, digs even deeper into the cultural landscape shaping this generation — and what it actually takes to disciple them well. Get it here.

This episode is a reminder that reaching the next generation was never going to look like reaching the last one — but the goal hasn't changed. It's still discipleship. It's just being fought for on a different battlefield.
