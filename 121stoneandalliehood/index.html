#121: The Lord’s Prayer Around the World with Stone and Allie Hood

November 19, 2026

On this episode of Missions Pulse, David Joannes sits down with Stone and Allie Hood, a married couple traveling the world to film believers praying the Lord’s Prayer in their own languages, a dream that began in a Hawaii dorm room ten years ago.

 📖 If this conversation stirs something in you, you can follow Stone and Allie’s journey, pray for them, and learn more about The Lord’s Prayer Project as they travel from nation to nation. 

Stone was twenty and studying at Kauai Bible College when a picture hit him on the walk back to his dorm: a wide variety of believers praying the Lord’s Prayer in their own language and culture, bouncing from person to person. He was overwhelmed. He ran to his room, journaled it, and felt a press to capture it. He bought a GoPro, later upgraded to a Sony, and signed up for the World Race, eleven countries in eleven months along the 10/40 Window. It seemed like the perfect chance. It didn’t work. Filming believers across a language barrier felt off, and Stone realized he was so focused on finishing the project that he wasn’t letting it change him. The dream went quiet for years.

Allie’s road ran through a different kind of waiting. She graduated from the University of Tennessee ready to sell everything and go anywhere, and God sent her to a corporate job in Denver instead. Stone followed her there and became a youth pastor. About a year into their marriage, he shared the dream with her, and she had an “aha moment”: this was what God had asked her to wait for. They held it for a couple more years until they felt a yes.

Those seasons reshaped how they pray. Allie says her prayers in the corporate job were mostly cries for help, which was beautiful, but she wasn’t good at listening and had no burning passion for intercession until God challenged her to notice how many of Jesus’ prayers were for others. Stone, as a youth pastor, ran into the Barna finding that the average pastor prays about five minutes a day and admitted it described him. He had realized he could do a lot without God, and “how scary that is.” The Lord’s Prayer, they say, can become hollow when we’ve known it all our lives.

David presses into what he calls a privatized faith, citing a 2013 Barna report that 51 percent of North American churchgoers don’t know what the Great Commission is. Stone and Allie agree that prayer is both a private pursuit and a corporate one, and they point out that the prayer begins with “our.” Prayer, they say, is absolutely missional: it starts with who God is, then moves to His kingdom come and His will be done. Allie shares how Luke 10:19 has fired her up to pray big, bold prayers, and how joining prayer sets in different countries has stretched her heart far beyond herself.

The heart of the conversation is a night in South Korea. Stone and Allie had no connections there but felt pressed to go. After a prayer set at a church, they felt no push to film the welcoming believers, even though they would have said yes. At the train station they discovered a cash-only kiosk, and they had just spent their last won at a Goodwill. They wondered if they should go back. Instead, they stopped and surrendered to God right there. Not even twenty seconds later, a Korean couple who had seen them praying walked up and asked in great English if they needed help. The man, Young, ended up saying the Lord’s Prayer in Korean, on camera, in the subway station. “This can only be you, God,” Stone says.

So far the trip has taken them through Japan, South Korea, and Thailand, with Indonesia, Kenya, and Estonia on their hearts, though they don’t know where they’re headed from one week to the next. The finished video will bounce clip by clip from English to Chinese to many other tongues, running perhaps two to four minutes. Along the way they’re asking believers three questions: What is prayer? How can someone live a devoted prayer life? How can the nations pray for your country? David adds a language lesson of his own, noting that the Chinese version of “Our Father” runs backwards, with “our” and “above the heavens” coming before “Father.” He ties it all to Revelation 7:9, where every tribe and tongue stands before the throne, with more than 17,000 languages and thousands of unreached people groups still waiting to hear.

Stone and Allie expect to finish filming in December and release the video on Instagram and their website in January or spring, though they’re walking open-handed. The conversation ends with Stone and Allie praying for anyone who feels stuck on the sidelines of God’s great dream, asking the Holy Spirit to teach all of us to pray.

This episode reminds us that prayer is the doorway into God’s heart for the nations. The invitation to know God’s heart and join His mission is still open.
